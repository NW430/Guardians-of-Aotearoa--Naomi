Our Environment

Aotearoa is home to unique ecosystems

Native Forests

New Zealand's native forests provide habitats for birds, insects, plants and other wildlife. Many of these species depend on forests for food, shelter and places to reproduce. Native trees also help prevent soil erosion, absorb carbon dioxide and maintain healthy ecosystems. Protecting forests helps preserve these habitats and supports the many species that rely on them.

Native Wildlife

Aotearoa has many unique native species, including kiwi, kererū, tūī and takahē. Many native animals have evolved in isolation, meaning they are found nowhere else in the world. However, some species are threatened by introduced predators, habitat loss and changes to their environment. Conservation efforts help protect these species and give their populations a better chance of recovering.

Waterways

Rivers, lakes and streams provide habitats for fish, insects, plants and other organisms. Healthy waterways are also important for people because they provide water for drinking, farming, recreation and other activities. Pollution, sediment and excessive nutrients can damage freshwater ecosystems. Protecting waterways helps maintain clean water and supports the organisms that depend on these environments.

Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms within an ecosystem, including animals, plants, fungi and microorganisms. New Zealand has a unique range of biodiversity because many of its species evolved in isolation and are found nowhere else in the world. Native birds such as kiwi, kererū and takahē are important parts of Aotearoa's natural environment.

New Zealand's biodiversity is threatened by introduced predators, habitat destruction, pollution and changes to the environment. When a species becomes threatened or disappears, it can affect other organisms because species are connected through food webs and ecosystems. Protecting biodiversity therefore helps maintain healthy and balanced ecosystems.

Protecting biodiversity is an important part of kaitiakitanga, which involves caring for and protecting the natural environment for future generations. By restoring native habitats, controlling introduced predators and protecting waterways, people can help preserve Aotearoa's unique species and ecosystems.

How can we protect it?

  • Protect native habitats
  • Control introduced predators
  • Reduce pollution
  • Plant native trees
  • Protect waterways
